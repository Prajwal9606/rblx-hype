In this world, at midnight, the street lanterns float down to the square and trade one wish for one memory.
You see, kids line up with moments to spare.
Bram traded a scraped knee.
He got a new bike.
Nora traded a rainy Tuesday.
She won the big race.
Toby traded his old scarf for a bag of gold.
And there's only one rule.
Give a memory you truly let go.
Otherwise the lantern takes the whole town's light.
But here's the weird part.
Nobody asks why the brightest lanterns feel completely empty inside.
And me, I'm the most forgetful kid in town.
I lose my left shoe every morning.
I leave my lunchbox on the porch.
Yesterday I walked to school in slippers.
Toby laughed right in my face.
He calls me Blank.
Now everyone calls me Blank.
Grandma always says one thing.
"An empty cup is the only one you can fill."
Then came the longest night of winter.
Toby stepped up to the giant king lantern in the square.
He wished to own the whole market.
He tried to trade a memory he still bragged about every day.
Crack!
The king lantern turned pitch black.
Every streetlamp in town snapped out.
Darkness swallowed the whole square.
Bram offered his biggest trophy.
It failed.
Nora offered her fastest sprint.
It failed.
Toby threw his bag of gold at the glass.
It failed.
"Do something!" Toby yelled.
Everyone stood shivering in the freezing dark.
That's when I understood.
They were holding onto their memories too tight.
Grandma's words echoed in my head.
"An empty cup is the only one you can fill."
I walked straight up to the black lantern.
I reached into my head and gave it my lost lunchbox.
Flicker!
A tiny golden spark woke up inside the glass.
So I gave it my missing left shoe.
Glow!
Then I gave it yesterday's slippers, my forgotten homework, and every Tuesday I couldn't remember anyway.
Up, up, up the flame shot into the sky!
Whoosh!
Every streetlamp snapped back on across the town.
Everyone stood in the warm glow, staring.
"Nice job, Blank," Toby whispered.
"Wait," he said. "Just Blank is fine."
Nobody called me Blank again.
Toby even helped me find my left shoe this morning.
Now I still forget where I put my lunchbox.
Though I can't remember why that bothered me.
