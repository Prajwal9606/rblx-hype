In this world, every kid gets a glass weather jar on their tenth birthday.

You see, weather jars do all the town's chores. Breeze kids dry laundry fast. Sun kids heat winter soup. Mist kids water whole gardens before breakfast.

And there's only one rule. Never bottle a storm cloud. A trapped storm grows furious and tears a town apart.

But here's the weird part. Every jar has a tiny brass vent. Nobody knows why.

And me, I got the worst jar in school. My lid came cracked right down the middle. So it only caught one tiny grey cloud. Just loaf-sized. It leaks a cold drizzle wherever I walk. Even indoors. Worse, whenever my little cloud drifts near another cloud, it steals all its water and dumps it straight on my shoes.

Bram has the biggest sun jar in town. He laughed when I soaked my lunch. He calls me Puddle. Now everyone calls me Puddle.

Grandpa always says one thing. "A storm isn't angry, kid. It's just too heavy to stay up." I never got it.

Then came the summer fair.

Bram wanted to prove he was the strongest kid in the plaza. He climbed the stone wall and shoved a giant black storm cloud into his jar.

Crack!

The glass split wide open. The storm burst out 100 feet tall. Pitch black. Lightning slammed the cobblestones.

The breeze kids blew at it hard. It failed. The storm only spun faster. The sun kids blasted it with heat. That failed too. The rain turned into blinding steam. Even the guards threw iron nets. Lightning snapped every rope in half.

Bram fell behind the plaza sign. Sparks rained down around him. He couldn't move.

That's when I understood. A storm isn't angry. It's just too heavy to stay up.

I sprinted into the wind and uncorked my cracked jar. My tiny grey cloud popped out. The gale slammed it straight back into my chest. I stepped closer. I held the cracked lid high.

My little cloud touched the storm's belly.

Whoosh!

It drank the whole sky. Down, down, down poured the water in one clean stream straight into the street drains. The thunder stopped. The black sky split into blue.

The whole town froze in the street. Nobody blinked. Bram walked over, soaking wet, and handed me a dry towel.

Now I work on the plaza roof every storm season. Nobody called me Puddle again. Well, except Bram, and now he brings me dry socks.
